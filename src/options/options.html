Privacy

  • All analysis runs locally in your browser. Page text is never sent to any server — there is no server.
  • No browsing history is collected or stored. The extension keeps nothing about the pages you visit beyond the counts shown in the popup while a tab is open.
  • No tracking, analytics, or ads.
  • Only your settings (toggles, thresholds, and site lists) are saved, in Chrome’s own storage (chrome.storage.sync), which Chrome may sync across your signed-in browsers.
  • The “Support on PayPal” button simply opens PayPal’s website in a new tab. The extension does not see, handle, or store any payment information.

Permissions

  • storage — save your settings.
  • activeTab + scripting — let the popup start the blocker on the current tab when that tab was opened before the extension was installed. Only used when you click the extension.
  • Content script on http/https pages — needed to read and hide page text locally.

How detection works — and its limits

Each block of text gets a confidence score from 0 to 100, built from several independent kinds of evidence:

  • Chatbot boilerplate — “As an AI language model”, “Certainly! Here are…”, leftover Markdown.
  • Stock phrasing & vocabulary — “delve into”, “plays a crucial role”, “tapestry”…
  • Formula — generic intros, “Moreover/Additionally” transitions, stock hedging, “In conclusion” wrap-ups.
  • Structure — “Label: explanation” bullet lists, template headings.
  • Rhythm — unusually uniform sentence and paragraph lengths.

Evidence of human writing (slang, casual typing, personal anecdotes, concrete names and numbers, varied rhythm) lowers the score. A single kind of evidence is never enough: text only reaches the “likely” range when at least two independent families agree.

Important: no tool can reliably prove that text was written by AI. Scores describe how closely text matches common AI writing patterns. Expect false positives (especially formal or templated human writing) and false negatives (edited or prompted-to-sound-human AI text). Use “Show content” freely, and allow-list sites where results aren’t useful.
